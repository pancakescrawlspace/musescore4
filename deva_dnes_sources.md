# Deva dnes (Bortniansky): sources

Research of 2026-10-23. The question: there is a Russian setting of
the Christmas troparion (or perhaps the kontakion) in an Italianate
style, from the 18th or 19th century. Who composed it? There is no
score of it here yet.

## Answer

Most likely **Dmitry Bortniansky** (1751–1825), *Дева днесь* ("Today the
Virgin gives birth"). This is the **kontakion** of Christmas, not the
troparion. The name rang a bell with the user, who accepted this answer.

## Reasoning

The candidates were the Russian composers of that period with an
Italian training, and the Italians who worked in Russia: Bortniansky,
Berezovsky, Vedel, Degtyarev, Galuppi and Sarti. Searches were run for
both Christmas texts with these names, in Russian and English: the
troparion «Рождество Твое, Христе Боже наш» (tone 4) and the kontakion
«Дева днесь». Two settings came up, both on the same recording,
*A Kremlin Christmas* (Capella of the Moscow Kremlin Museum, Gennady
Dmitriak): Bortniansky's kontakion and Degtyarev's troparion.

Bortniansky fits the description best:

- **The Italian style.** He studied at the Imperial Court Chapel under
  Baldassare Galuppi, who took him to Italy in 1769. There he wrote
  operas for Venice (*Creonte*, 1776; *Alcide*, 1778) and Modena
  (*Quinto Fabio*, 1779). He returned to St Petersburg in 1779 and
  directed the Court Chapel from 1796 to 1825. Musica Russica calls him
  "trained in the Italian compositional style of his epoch".
- **The piece is well known.** It is in the repertoire of the Mariinsky
  chorus ("Kontakion of Christmas (Today the Virgin...)"), in the OCA's
  music for the Nativity ("Kontakion (Bortniansky)"), published by
  Musica Russica (Bo033, SSAATTBB, Church Slavonic; it calls the harmony
  "sumptuous", with doubled sopranos and tenors) and freely available
  from the Mutopia Project.
- **The text.** The kontakion goes back to St Romanos the Melodist (6th
  century).

### The alternative: Degtyarev's troparion

If the troparion was meant after all, the candidate is **Stepan
Degtyarev** (1766–1813), Bortniansky's contemporary. The *Kremlin
Christmas* album lists his "Troparion: 'Thy Birth, Christ, Our Lord'"
(1:18). Only that track listing was found: no score and no description
of its style. So nothing confirms that it is Italianate.

### Not found or ruled out

- No setting of either text by Berezovsky, Vedel, Galuppi or Sarti came
  up in the searches.
- Other settings that did come up are later and not Italianate:
  Arkhangelsky's «Рождество Твое» (CPDL) and Sviridov's *Christmas
  Troparion*, No. 4. Sviridov's is 20th-century, in the chordal style of
  common chant.
- Not checked: the composition date of Bortniansky's kontakion, and
  Degtyarev's training.
