# Свете тихий (Znamenny): the melodic line

Analysis of 2026-10-23 of `svete_tihij_znamennyj.musicxml` (azbyka.ru's
Vespers series, no. 09_1; see `znamenny_sources.md`). The question: the
melody seems to start on the subdominant and end on the supertonic of F
major. Is that typical, or should it end on the tonic?

## Answer

In F-major terms that reading is correct, but the melody isn't in F major.
Its central note, its **final**, is G, and ending there is normal.

| | |
|---|---|
| range | E4–B♭4, five notes; E occurs once |
| time per pitch (quarter notes) | **G 88**, A 67, B♭ 33, F 19, E 2 |
| ends of the 8 lines | A A A G G A G G |
| long notes (wholes) at caesuras | all on **G** ("ти**хий**", "небесна**го**", "Хри**сте**", "вечер**ний**", "Бо**га**", "Бо**жий**", "живот да**яй**", "**мир**") |
| first / last note | B♭ / G |

G is where the melody spends most of its time, where it pauses at every
caesura, and where it ends. The melody moves in the narrow band G–A–B♭,
with F below as a neighbour. It typically starts at the top (B♭) and
settles down onto G. The opening B♭ is a starting tone, not a subdominant.

**The key signature:** one flat only says that B is B♭. Together with the
single E natural, the notes form G A B♭ C D E F: in Western terms
**G Dorian**, not F major. Major/minor function (tonic, subdominant)
doesn't apply to monophonic chant. What matters is the final, the range
and the small set of notes the melody returns to.

## Is it typical?

**Finals other than the tonic of the key signature.** Other settings in the
same series, read the same way (from the vector shapes of the PDFs). The
note names are as written; their key signatures were not checked.

| setting | final |
|---|---|
| «Свете тихий», tone 3 (09_3) | E |
| «Свете тихий», tone 6 (09_4) | D |
| «Господи воззвах», tone 4 (08_4) | D |
| «Господи воззвах», tone 8 (08_8) | C |
| «Богородице Дево», Kirillo-Belozersky manuscript (16) | G |

The final depends on the tone (*glas*) and the chant, not on a "key".

**Background, from general knowledge, not checked against a source here:**
- Znamenny chant is built from short melodic formulas (*popevki*) within a
  small note set.
- In modern transcriptions, G with B♭ is a common central trichord.
- Harmonized in the obikhod, a final G is usually set as a G minor or G
  major chord, sometimes E♭; such endings therefore still sound
  "finished" to modern ears.

**Caveat:** the figures for the other settings come from automatic reading,
checked for plausibility only, not note by note.

## Does tone 6 mean G Dorian?

No. The melody analysed here (09_1) carries no tone label; the series'
tone-6 «Свете тихий» is a different file (09_4). As written, that one
ends on D and centres on D, E and F. So "G Dorian" describes this one
melody's note set and final, not a tone.

More generally (from general knowledge, not checked against a source
here), a Russian tone (*glas*) is a family of melodic formulas
(*popevki*) and cadences, not a scale. Its notes and final depend on the
chant (Znamenny, Kievan, Greek, obikhod) and the kind of hymn (stichera,
troparia, irmoi, prokeimena). The eight tones don't map one-to-one onto
the eight Western church modes. In the repository's obikhod tones (Heer,
ik roep) the tones are written mostly in F major regardless of the tone.
