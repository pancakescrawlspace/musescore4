# Heer, ik roep (heer_ik_roep_t1–t8): sources

Findings of 2026-10-23, tracing the melodies of the eight tones of "Heer,
ik roep tot U" (Ps. 140/141, Slavonic «Господи, воззвах»), the Dutch
versions in `heer_ik_roep_t1.mscz` … `heer_ik_roep_t8.mscz`. The Dutch
adaptation itself is not identified yet; see "Open questions".

## Method

Melodies were compared by scale degree, not by pitch, so that a
transposition doesn't hide a match. The scores were read from the
`.mscz` (soprano and bass per measure) and compared by eye with the
reference PDFs, measure by measure, at the start of each tone ("Heer ik
roep tot U, verhoor mij; verhoor mij, o Heer"). That is not yet a
note-for-note comparison of whole tones.

The eight tones here are in F major, except tone 6 in G major. All are
four-part on two staves, reciting tones with cadence formulas.

## Result: the OCA's "Common Chant" (Court chant, after L'vov/Bakhmetev)

The closest match is the Orthodox Church in America's "Lord, I Call" in
"Common Chant, arr. from L'vov/Bakhmetev", i.e. the Russian Court chant
(*придворный напев*) of the Imperial Court Chapel, as adapted by the OCA
to English texts.

- On the OCA's download pages "Lord I call…" is listed as "Obikhod"; the
  PDFs themselves say "Common Chant, arr. from L'vov/Bakhmetev".
- The PDFs, tones [1](https://www.oca.org/PDF/Music/Tone1/t1-lic-obikhod-tt.pdf), [2](https://www.oca.org/PDF/Music/Tone2/t2-lic-obikhod-tt.pdf), [3](https://www.oca.org/PDF/Music/Tone3/t3-lic-obikhod-tt.pdf), [4](https://www.oca.org/PDF/Music/Tone4/t4-lic-obikhod-tt.pdf), [5](https://www.oca.org/PDF/Music/Tone5/t5-lic-obikhod-tt.pdf), [6](https://www.oca.org/PDF/Music/Tone6/t6-lic-obikhod-tt.pdf), [7](https://www.oca.org/PDF/Music/Tone7/t7-lic-obikhod-tt.pdf), [8](https://www.oca.org/PDF/Music/Tone8/t8-lic-obikhod-tt.pdf)
  (`-yy` instead of `-tt` in the file name is the other layout of the same
  music).
- The OCA's download pages per tone: [1](https://www.oca.org/liturgics/music-downloads/tone-1), [2](https://www.oca.org/liturgics/music-downloads/tone-2), [3](https://www.oca.org/liturgics/music-downloads/tone-3), [4](https://www.oca.org/liturgics/music-downloads/tone-4), [5](https://www.oca.org/liturgics/music-downloads/tone-5), [6](https://www.oca.org/liturgics/music-downloads/tone-6), [7](https://www.oca.org/liturgics/music-downloads/tone-7), [8](https://www.oca.org/liturgics/music-downloads/tone-8).

Per tone, at the start:

| tone | match with the OCA Common Chant |
|---|---|
| 1 | Same melody, recited on the 4th degree (B♭); "verhoor mij, o Heer" A–A–B♭–B♭–G against "Hear me, O Lord" A–B♭–B♭–G. Own ornament: "Heer ik roep tot U, verhoor mij" ends G–A–G–F over D in the bass, the OCA on A. |
| 2 | Unclear. Both recite on B♭ with upper neighbours C/D, but the OCA harmonizes in G minor (bass G, F♯), the Dutch version in F major (bass C/F); cadences differ. |
| 3 | Same melody with an added B♭ neighbour note; the cadence "verhoor mij, o Heer" A–G–F–E over F–C–G–C is identical. |
| 4 | Melody and bass almost identical (G…A A │ B♭ B♭ B♭ A │ C B♭ A…), differing only with the number of syllables. |
| 5 | Unclear. The Dutch top voice recites on G (over C), the OCA soprano on B♭; perhaps a different voicing, perhaps a different version. |
| 6 | Same melody and key (G major): G…F♯ G │ A A G G A against "Hear me, O Lord" A G G A. |
| 7 | Same cadences (ending F–E over G–C); the first phrase partly different. |
| 8 | Same melody: A G G … A.–G F │ A A B♭ A G against A B♭ A. |

The keys agree too (F major, tone 6 G major), which points to a direct
connection with the OCA's settings rather than only the same chant
tradition.

## Bakhmetev's Obikhod (1869): the Court chant itself

- *Обиход нотнаго церковнаго пения при высочайшем дворе употребляемый*,
  ч. 1 (Всенощное бдение), ed. N. Bakhmetev, St Petersburg, Court Chapel,
  1869: a scan on [Eastern Church Music Resources, scanned chant books](https://ecmr.fi/scannedchantbooks.html)
  (`Scanned_Chantbooks/Obihod1869Bahm1.pdf`; the site was down on
  2026-10-23 and the file was fetched through the
  [Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://ecmr.fi/scannedchantbooks.html)).
  Vol. 2 is `Obihod1869Bahm2.pdf` there. Library record:
  [Книжный памятник РГБ № 33912](https://knpam.rusneb.ru/kp/item33912).
- "Господи воззвах, 8-ми гласов со стихиры и Богородичны" is on printed
  pp. 5–29 (PDF page = printed page + 4): tone 1 p. 5, 2 p. 8, 3 p. 11,
  4 p. 14, 5 p. 18, 6 p. 21, 7 p. 24, 8 p. 27. Open score, four staves
  (soprano, alto, tenor clefs and bass clef), no key signatures.
- Tones 1 and 2 there are the melodies of the azbyka.ru "Обиход" set and
  the 1914 Court Obikhod (below): tone 1 recites on the 2nd degree (D in
  C), tone 2 recites and then rises a third before the cadence. So that
  azbyka.ru set is Bakhmetev's Court chant, and the OCA's tones 1 and 2,
  despite "arr. from L'vov/Bakhmetev", are not Bakhmetev's "Господи
  воззвах" — nor are the Dutch ones. Tone 3 also looks different by its
  contour; tones 4–8 are not yet compared.
- L'vov's own Obikhod (1848) has not been found online yet.

## Ruled out

- **The azbyka.ru "Обиход" set**:
  [Господи, воззвах гласы 1–8 для смешанного хора](https://azbyka.ru/kliros/noty-pesnopenij-gospodi-vozzvah-glasy-1-8-dlya-smeshannogo-hora/)
  (tone 1 in G major). It turned out to be Bakhmetev's Court chant (see
  above). Tones 1 and 2 are different melodies from the Dutch ones: tone 1
  recites on the 2nd degree, the Dutch version on the 4th.
- **The 1914 Court Obikhod**, "Господи воззвах, придворный напев (1914)",
  a scan of an old edition, on [djuKliros: Octoechos / Гласы](https://www.djukliros.com/octoechos-%D0%B3%D0%BB%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%8B)
  ([PDF on Google Drive](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1oRzhP6P_ELtLYkSjQFdhvKzYBculR1F7)).
  Its tone 1 (G major) is the same melody as the azbyka.ru Obikhod set
  above, so not the OCA's tone 1, nor the Dutch one. Only tones 1 and 4
  were looked at; tone 4 is in G major there (F in the Dutch version), and
  its first system wasn't compared.
- **Kievan chant** and **Serbian chant**, tone 1, from the OCA:
  [Kievan (arr. B. Ledkovsky)](https://www.oca.org/PDF/Music/Tone1/t1-lic-kievan-tt.pdf),
  [Serbian (arr. P. Drobac)](https://www.oca.org/PDF/Music/Tone1/LIC-T1-serb-tt.pdf):
  different melodies.
- **Smirnov's men's-choir settings** (azbyka.ru,
  [Воскресные стихиры на Господи воззвах](https://azbyka.ru/kliros/voskresnye-stihiry-na-gospodi-vozzvah-noty-dlya-muzhskogo-hora/)):
  tone 1 "ред. Г. Смирнова", tone 2 in Lavra chant and in shortened
  Kievan chant. Only glanced at, not compared in detail.

## Open questions

1. A note-for-note comparison of all eight tones with the OCA Common
   Chant, listing exactly where the Dutch version deviates; tones 2 and 5
   especially.
2. Where the OCA's "arr. from L'vov/Bakhmetev" tones come from, since in
   tones 1 and 2 they are not Bakhmetev's "Господи воззвах" (1869).
   Perhaps from his stichera melodies, from L'vov's Obikhod (1848, not
   found online yet), or from a later adaptation. Bakhmetev's tones 3–8
   still need comparing; automatic note reading of the scan proved too
   unreliable, so that means reading the C clefs by eye.
3. Who made the Dutch adaptation, and from what: directly from the OCA's
   English settings, or from a common (Russian or other) source. A Dutch
   parish book or the choir's own sheets would settle it.
