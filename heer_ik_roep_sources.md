# Heer, ik roep (heer_ik_roep_t1–t8): sources

Findings of 2026-10-23, tracing the melodies of the eight tones of "Heer,
ik roep tot U" (Ps. 140/141, Slavonic «Господи, воззвах»), the Dutch
versions in `heer_ik_roep_t1.mscz` … `heer_ik_roep_t8.mscz`. The Dutch
adaptation itself is not identified yet; see "Open questions".

## Method

Melodies were compared by scale degree, not by pitch, so that a
transposition doesn't hide a match. The scores were read from the
`.mscz` (soprano and bass per measure) and compared by eye with the
reference PDFs, measure by measure, at the start of each tone ("Heer ik
roep tot U, verhoor mij; verhoor mij, o Heer"). That is not yet a
note-for-note comparison of whole tones.

The eight tones here are in F major, except tone 6 in G major. All are
four-part on two staves, reciting tones with cadence formulas.

## Result: Bakhmetev's Court chant, in the OCA's close-harmony setting

The chant is the Court chant (*придворный напев*) of the L'vov-Bakhmetev
Obikhod, and the harmonization is closest to the Orthodox Church in
America's "Common Chant" settings of "Lord, I Call".

**The melody is in the alto.** In the Dutch version, as in the OCA's
settings, the soprano runs a third above the alto, and the chant melody
is the alto line. The OCA's chanting tutorial says so of its own settings
("transposed and in 'narrow' or 'closed' harmony, and the melody is found
in the alto voice"), while Bakhmetev gives the melody to the top voice.
An earlier comparison of the Dutch soprano with Bakhmetev's soprano
wrongly concluded that they differ.

Bakhmetev's melody (his top voice, read by eye from the 1869 scan) against
the Dutch version, first phrase of each tone ("Господи воззвах к Тебе,
услыши мя; услыши мя, Господи" / "Heer, ik roep tot U, verhoor mij;
verhoor mij, o Heer"), by scale degree (Bakhmetev without key signature,
read in C; the Dutch version in F, tone 6 in G):

| tone | Bakhmetev (1869) | Dutch version | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | D…D B C. C C (2…2 7 1) | alto G (A) G…G E F E D (2…2 7 1 7 6) | same melody, in the alto; Dutch adds a falling ending |
| 2 | D…D F E D │ D D D D (2…2 4 3 2) | alto G (A) G…G F G A A G (2…1 2 3 3 2) | differs: Bakhmetev (and the OCA's alto, G…B♭ A G) rise a third, the Dutch only a second; the Dutch harmony (F major) differs from the OCA's (G minor) too |
| 3 | F…F F E (recitation, cadence a step down) | alto E F…F G F E D E | same recitation and cadence (as the OCA's alto F…F E), with Dutch additions: an E before, a G neighbour, E–D–E at the close |
| 4 | B…B C. C C │ D D D D D D C (7…1 │ 2…2 1) | alto E…E F F │ G G G G F | same melody, in the alto |
| 5 | D…D E B C │ E E E E D D C (2…3 7 1 │ 3…2 2 1) | soprano G…G (F G) A F │ A A G F G (2…3 1 │ 3 3 2 1 2) | same melody, but in the **soprano**; the OCA keeps it in the alto (so its soprano recites on B♭) |
| 6 | D…D C♯ D D │ E E E E E D E | alto E…E D♯ E │ F♯ F♯ E E F♯ (G major) | same melody, in the alto |
| 7 | C D…D C. C B │ B D B B A A G (as far as legible) | soprano G…G F G A F G │ A B♭ A G F E; alto a third lower | unclear: the scan is hard to read here, and neither Dutch voice follows it closely |
| 8 | F E…E F E D │ F F F F F G F E (1 7…7 1 7 6 │ 1…2 1 7) | alto F E…E F E D │ F F G F E | same melody, in the alto |

Only the first phrase of each tone was compared; the later phrases and
the bass lines are still open.

### The OCA's settings

- The OCA's singing tutorial, [Learning the Tones](https://www.oca.org/liturgics/learning-the-tones):
  a self-study course in the eight tones of Common (L'vov-Bakhmetev
  Obikhod) and Kievan chant, for now only the stichera melodies (psalm
  verses, troparia and kanons are announced, not there yet). Per tone an
  explanation PDF and recordings: unison, full choir, and each voice
  alone. The Common Chant explanations, tones [1](https://media.oca.org/chanting-tutorial/Tutorial-Intro-Obikhod-Tone1-Explanation.pdf), [2](https://media.oca.org/chanting-tutorial/Tutorial-Obikhod-Tone2-Explanation.pdf), [3](https://media.oca.org/chanting-tutorial/Tutorial-Common-Tone3-Explanation.pdf), [4](https://media.oca.org/chanting-tutorial/Tutorial-Obikhod-Tone4-Explanation.pdf), [5](https://media.oca.org/chanting-tutorial/Tutorial-Common-Tone5-Explanation.pdf), [6](https://media.oca.org/chanting-tutorial/Tutorial-Common-Tone6-Explanation.pdf), [7](https://media.oca.org/chanting-tutorial/Tutorial-Common-Tone7-Explanation.pdf), [8](https://media.oca.org/chanting-tutorial/Tutorial-Obikhod-Tone8-Explanation.pdf)

Compared with the Dutch version at the start of each tone (soprano and
bass; both settings are in the same close harmony):

| tone | match with the OCA setting |
|---|---|
| 1 | Same melody and harmony; own ornament: "Heer ik roep tot U, verhoor mij" ends over D in the bass, the OCA on F. |
| 2 | Unclear: the OCA harmonizes in G minor (bass G, F♯), the Dutch version in F major (bass C/F); cadences differ. |
| 3 | Same, with an added B♭ neighbour note in the soprano; the cadence "verhoor mij, o Heer" (A–G–F–E over F–C–G–C) is identical. |
| 4 | Melody and bass almost identical, differing only with the number of syllables. |
| 5 | Unclear: the Dutch soprano recites on G, the OCA soprano on B♭. |
| 6 | Same melody, harmony and key (G major). |
| 7 | Same cadences; the first phrase partly different. |
| 8 | Same melody and harmony. |

The keys agree too (F major, tone 6 G major), which points to the OCA's
settings, or their source, as the model of the Dutch version.

## Bakhmetev's Obikhod (1869)

- *Обиход нотнаго церковнаго пения при высочайшем дворе употребляемый*,
  ч. 1 (Всенощное бдение), ed. N. Bakhmetev, St Petersburg, Court Chapel,
  1869: a scan on [Eastern Church Music Resources, scanned chant books](https://ecmr.fi/scannedchantbooks.html)
  (`Scanned_Chantbooks/Obihod1869Bahm1.pdf`; the site was down on
  2026-10-23 and the file was fetched through the
  [Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://ecmr.fi/scannedchantbooks.html)).
  Vol. 2 is `Obihod1869Bahm2.pdf` there. Library record:
  [Книжный памятник РГБ № 33912](https://knpam.rusneb.ru/kp/item33912).
- "Господи воззвах, 8-ми гласов со стихиры и Богородичны" is on printed
  pp. 5–29 (PDF page = printed page + 4): tone 1 p. 5, 2 p. 8, 3 p. 11,
  4 p. 14, 5 p. 18, 6 p. 21, 7 p. 24, 8 p. 27. Open score, four staves
  (soprano, alto, tenor clefs and bass clef), no key signatures, the
  melody in the top voice.
- The same melodies, with the melody in the soprano, are in the 1914
  Court Obikhod and the azbyka.ru "Обиход" set below (checked for tones 1
  and 2).
- L'vov's own Obikhod (1848) has not been found online; since the OCA
  names the 1869 edition as its source, it isn't needed for this.

## Ruled out

- **Kievan chant** and **Serbian chant**, tone 1, from the OCA:
  [Kievan (arr. B. Ledkovsky)](https://www.oca.org/PDF/Music/Tone1/t1-lic-kievan-tt.pdf),
  [Serbian (arr. P. Drobac)](https://www.oca.org/PDF/Music/Tone1/LIC-T1-serb-tt.pdf):
  different melodies.
- **Smirnov's men's-choir settings** (azbyka.ru,
  [Воскресные стихиры на Господи воззвах](https://azbyka.ru/kliros/voskresnye-stihiry-na-gospodi-vozzvah-noty-dlya-muzhskogo-hora/)):
  tone 1 "ред. Г. Смирнова", tone 2 in Lavra chant and in shortened
  Kievan chant. Only glanced at, not compared in detail.

## Searched without result

- The Dutch text ("Heer, ik roep tot U, verhoor mij", with "toon",
  "noten", "vespers", "orthodoxe parochie"): no Dutch scores found online.
- `~/dev/liturgie` (the retypeset Dutch liturgy on this computer) holds
  only the Divine Liturgy, no vespers.
- [kpds42.ru: Всенощное бдение](https://kpds42.ru/pages/vsenoshchnoe-bdenie)
  has no "Господи воззвах".
- Automatic note reading of the Bakhmetev scan (a small script with
  scipy) was too unreliable on the skewed 1869 print with C clefs (hollow
  notes missed, notes off by a step); the comparisons above are by eye.
