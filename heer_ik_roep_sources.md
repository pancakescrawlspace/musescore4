# Heer, ik roep (heer_ik_roep_t1–t8): sources

Findings of 2026-10-23, tracing the melodies of the eight tones of "Heer,
ik roep tot U" (Ps. 140/141, Slavonic «Господи, воззвах»), the Dutch
versions in `heer_ik_roep_t1.mscz` … `heer_ik_roep_t8.mscz`. The Dutch
adaptation itself is not identified yet; see "Open questions".

## Result: the Moscow obikhod tones (current Moscow parish practice)

The Dutch tones are the stichera tones of the **contemporary Moscow
obikhod** («современная московская традиция»), in the four-part
close harmony of Moscow parish choirs, with soprano and alto in parallel
thirds. The best witness is E. Kustovsky and N. Potemkina, *Пособие по
изучению осмогласия современной московской традиции* (Moscow: Church of
the Three Hierarchs at Kulishki, 1999): its stichera tones ("А.
Стихирный") match the Dutch in all four voices, note for note, in tones
1, 2, 6 and 8, and with only a few passing notes in 3, 4, 5 and 7 (see
"The Moscow obikhod"). Tones 1, 2, 4 and 6 are in the Dutch key; 3, 5, 7
and 8 are a tone higher there (G for the Dutch F).

So the earlier findings below are refined, not overturned: the "Court
chant in the OCA's setting" (tones 1, 3, 4, 6, 8) and the "Kievan chant"
(2, 7) are both part of this Moscow tradition, whose melodies the
handbook traces to the «Круг церковных песнопений обычного напева
Московской епархии» (1882) with influence of the St Petersburg Court
chant; the OCA's settings and Smirnov's are close relatives, the
Moscow handbook is closer than either.

## Method

Melodies were compared by scale degree, not by pitch, so that a
transposition doesn't hide a match. The scores were read from the
`.mscz` (soprano and bass per measure) and compared by eye with the
reference PDFs, measure by measure, at the start of each tone ("Heer ik
roep tot U, verhoor mij; verhoor mij, o Heer"). That is not yet a
note-for-note comparison of whole tones.

The eight tones here are in F major, except tone 6 in G major. All are
four-part on two staves, reciting tones with cadence formulas.

## Earlier result: Bakhmetev's Court chant, in the OCA's close-harmony setting

(Refined by "The Moscow obikhod" below.)

The chant is the Court chant (*придворный напев*) of the L'vov-Bakhmetev
Obikhod, and the harmonization is closest to the Orthodox Church in
America's "Common Chant" settings of "Lord, I Call".

**The melody is in the alto.** In the Dutch version, as in the OCA's
settings, the soprano runs a third above the alto, and the chant melody
is the alto line. The OCA's chanting tutorial says so of its own settings
("transposed and in 'narrow' or 'closed' harmony, and the melody is found
in the alto voice"), while Bakhmetev gives the melody to the top voice.
An earlier comparison of the Dutch soprano with Bakhmetev's soprano
wrongly concluded that they differ.

Bakhmetev's melody (his top voice, read by eye from the 1869 scan) against
the Dutch version, first phrase of each tone ("Господи воззвах к Тебе,
услыши мя; услыши мя, Господи" / "Heer, ik roep tot U, verhoor mij;
verhoor mij, o Heer"), by scale degree (Bakhmetev without key signature,
read in C; the Dutch version in F, tone 6 in G):

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | D…D B C. C C (2…2 7 1) | alto G (A) G…G E F E D (2…2 7 1 7 6) | same melody, in the alto; Dutch adds a falling ending |
| 2 | D…D F E D │ D D D D (2…2 4 3 2) | alto G (A) G…G F G A A G (2…1 2 3 3 2) | differs: Bakhmetev (and the OCA's alto, G…B♭ A G) rise a third, the Dutch only a second; the Dutch harmony (F major) differs from the OCA's (G minor) too |
| 3 | F…F F E (recitation, cadence a step down) | alto E F…F G F E D E | same recitation and cadence (as the OCA's alto F…F E), with Dutch additions: an E before, a G neighbour, E–D–E at the close |

Only the first phrase of each tone was compared; the later phrases and
the bass lines are still open.

So the Dutch version is the OCA's setting in tones 1, 3, 4, 6 and 8 (with
added turns at the cadences in 1, 3 and 6; Smirnov's men's-choir edition
of the same setting has those in tone 1 and partly in tone 3, see
"Smirnov's men's-choir settings"). Tones 2, 5 and 7 are not the
Court chant: 2 and 7 are the Kievan chant, 5 a mixture (see "Tones 2, 5
and 7: Kievan chant").

The keys agree too (F major, tone 6 G major), which points to the OCA's
settings, or their source, as the model of the Dutch version.

## Tones 2, 5 and 7: Kievan chant

The three tones that are not the OCA's setting turned out to come from
another chant: the **Kievan chant** (*Киевский распев*) as Russian
parishes commonly sing "Господи воззвах", not the Court chant.

The witness found: *Обиход церковного пения*, ч. 1, Храм святителя Тихона,
Архангельск 2005 (a parish obikhod; music typeset by Д. В. Аллеманов):
[PDF on kliros.ru](http://kliros.ru/noty/obihod1_arhangelsk.pdf),
section "Образцы напевов на «Господи воззвах»: Киевский распев", tones
1–8 on pp. 8–15 (tone 2 p. 9, F major; tone 5 p. 12, G major; tone 7
p. 14, G major), each with its "Запев" (the refrain "И ныне, и присно…").
Soprano, alto and bass on two staves; the bass staff has one line. Its
notes were read from the PDF (Sibelius, Opus font) and compared with the
Dutch soprano, alto and bass, transposed where the key differs:

- **Tone 2: the Kievan chant, same key.** Soprano, alto and bass agree
  note for note from the start (soprano B♭ C B♭ A B♭ C B♭ A B♭ D C B♭ A…,
  bass C F C F…); the Dutch version's F major harmony (where the OCA has
  G minor) comes from here.
- **Tone 7: the Kievan chant**, a tone lower (Kievan in G, Dutch in F).
  Soprano 2…2 1 2 3 3 2 │ 3 4 3 2 2 1 1 7 against the Dutch 2…2 1 2 3 1 2 │
  3 4 3 2 1 7; the bass practically identical (one G2 for G3); the Dutch
  adds an occasional passing note. So the Dutch tone 7 is not a weak
  adaptation of the Court chant's tone 7 (which rises 7–1–2 into its
  reciting note and falls back, as in the OCA's alto), but the Kievan
  chant, which recites plainly with a small turn before each cadence.
- **Tone 5: a mixture.** The Dutch bass follows the Kievan bass outline
  (C…F │ F…C │ C…D) and shares its cadence on C♯–D at "verhoor mij", but
  the melody differs: the Kievan recites a third higher (on A, in F), the
  Dutch soprano on G, which is closer to Bakhmetev's Court-chant melody
  (see the table below).

The comparisons are made with `references/tools/cmp_ref.py` (`python3
cmp_ref.py PDF PAGE TONE SEMITONES`).

## Smirnov's men's-choir settings (azbyka.ru)

*На Господи воззвах стихиры воскресные*, for men's choir (T1, T2 on a
treble staff an octave down; baritone and bass), "Ред. Г. Смирнова",
"© 2021. Georgy Smirnov. Arrangement and typing" (the PDFs were made on
2021-11-27), on azbyka.ru:
[Воскресные стихиры на Господи воззвах](https://azbyka.ru/kliros/voskresnye-stihiry-na-gospodi-vozzvah-noty-dlya-muzhskogo-hora/).
Tones 1 and 3–8 are Smirnov's edition of the Court chant; for tone 2 the
page has the Lavra chant (*лаврский напев*) and the shortened Kievan chant
(*сокращённый киевский распев*) instead. Each PDF has the psalm verses on
page 1 ("Господи воззвах … Да исправится … услыши мя, Господи") and the
verse refrains with the stichera texts on page 2; only page 1 has a
Dutch counterpart.

Compared with the Dutch version note by note, all four voices
(2026-10-23), with `references/tools/cmp_smirnov.py` (`python3
cmp_smirnov.py TONE [PDF]`). The PDFs are vector files (Finale, Jazz font),
read like the OCA's. Men's and mixed voices differ in range, so voices
are compared by pitch class, repeats merged, after transposing:
Smirnov writes in C (tone 1 B♭, tone 6 F) where the Dutch is in F (tone 6
G). The voices correspond one to one: T1 is the Dutch soprano, T2 the
alto, baritone the tenor, bass the bass; in tone 6, T1 is the alto, T2
the tenor and the baritone the soprano (an octave down). The same
measure was taken for the OCA's settings and the Kievan chant
(Arkhangelsk obikhod). "Covers" is the share of the Dutch notes the
setting has, in the same order; "match" (difflib's ratio) also counts the
setting's notes that the Dutch doesn't have. So covers 1.00 with a lower
match means: every Dutch note, plus extra notes.

Per tone (measure numbers are the Dutch ones):

- **Tone 1: closer than the OCA.** Smirnov has the cadential turns at
  "verhoor mij" (m1, m5: soprano B♭–C … A–G–F, alto G–A … F–E–D) that the
  OCA lacks; the Dutch soprano and alto are Smirnov's T1 and T2 but for
  one A–G (F–E) in m2. The Dutch bass adds C–F in m1, m5 and m7, which
  Smirnov doesn't have either; tenor m7: Smirnov C♯–D, the Dutch C.
- **Tone 2: the shortened Kievan chant is the Dutch tone 2.** Smirnov's
  T1 is the Dutch alto note for note (50 notes, no difference), his bass
  has every note of the Dutch bass (and a few more F/B♭); the middle
  voices are arranged differently (three men's voices for the Dutch
  soprano and tenor). It is closer than the Arkhangelsk obikhod's Kievan
  chant (0.70 of the alto and soprano), which agrees with the Dutch only
  at the start. The Lavra chant is unrelated.
- **Tone 3: closer than the OCA in the upper voices.** Smirnov has the G
  before each first phrase and a turn at "verhoor mij", but B♭–A–G–A
  (alto G–F–E–F) where the Dutch has B♭–A–G–F (G–F–E–D) (m1, 3, 5, 7, 9);
  his baritone is the Dutch tenor exactly (the OCA's differs in m2,
  m10–11). His bass adds E–F neighbour notes; the OCA's bass is closer.
- **Tone 4: the OCA is closer.** Smirnov has every Dutch note but adds
  passing notes (soprano G–A, B♭–G–F in m3–4 and m9–10) where the OCA and
  the Dutch agree; only the Dutch tenor's C♯ at the final cadence (which
  the OCA lacks) is in Smirnov too.
- **Tone 5: Smirnov explains it.** Like the Dutch, and unlike the OCA,
  Smirnov has the melody in the top voice, reciting on degree 2, and his
  bass has every note of the Dutch bass. The differences: the Dutch
  soprano has F for Smirnov's A in m2, 5, 8, adds E and E–D in m3 and m9,
  and ends the final cadence (m6, m11) a third lower (E–D for G–F); the
  Dutch tenor adds G–F in m3, 6, 9, 11. So tone 5 is not a mixture of the
  Court and Kievan chants, but the Court chant in a setting like
  Smirnov's.
- **Tone 6: closer than the OCA.** Smirnov has the Dutch alto's D♯ in m3
  (the OCA D); the turn at the final cadence (m6, m11: soprano A, alto F♯,
  bass D added) is in neither.
- **Tone 7: still the Kievan chant.** Smirnov's upper voices are nearer
  the Dutch than the OCA's (0.69 against 0.24) and about as near as the
  Kievan chant, but they differ from m4 to m7, and his bass is far off;
  the Kievan bass remains the best match.
- **Tone 8: the OCA is closer.** Smirnov has every Dutch note but adds
  turns (soprano G–A–B♭–A–G in m2 and m8, G–A–B♭ in m5), which the OCA and
  the Dutch don't have; the Dutch tenor's C♯ at the final cadence is in
  Smirnov.

So Smirnov's settings are the same close-harmony arrangement of the Court
chant as the OCA's (the melody in the line that is the Dutch alto, except
in tone 5, where it is on top as in the Dutch), but a different branch of
it: in tones 1, 3, 5 and 6 the Dutch shares details
with Smirnov that the OCA lacks, in tones 4 and 8 the other way round.
The azbyka.ru files date from 2021, but the arrangements are older (men's
choir 2013, mixed choir 2007; see the next section), and in tone 2
Smirnov's close-harmony setting for mixed choir is the Dutch exactly, in
the same key. For the other tones his keys differ from the Dutch ones
(which are the OCA's), so the Dutch is not simply a copy of his 2007
set; more likely both go back to the same Russian practice version of
the obikhod, with these cadence turns, the melody on top in tone 5 and
the shortened Kievan chant for tone 2.

### What Smirnov's settings are based on (2026-10-23)

Georgy Smirnov (b. 1960; Gnesin college 1980, Moscow Conservatory 1985)
leads the men's choir «Православные Певчие» and was precentor at the
Danilov Monastery and at the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour
([precentor.ru](https://precentor.ru/s_g/info_r.html), also profundo.ru).
His "Библиотека регента" has two Octoechos sets with "Господи воззвах":

- [Октоих для мужского хора](https://precentor.ru/s_g/octoechos.html):
  "Редакция и составление Г.Смирнова", Moscow 2013–2015, Trinity Church
  in Ostankino (patriarchal metochion); "© Arrangement and typing by
  Georgy Smirnov in 2013". The azbyka.ru files (2021) are these
  arrangements re-typeset: tone 1 of 2013 matches the Dutch exactly as
  the 2021 file does. Smirnov labels tone 4 "(Обиход)"; the tone 3
  contents page says "Лаврского напева".
- [Октоих для смешанного хора](https://precentor.ru/s_g/octoechos_mix.html),
  Cathedral of Christ the Saviour, 2007 (no tone 4): tone 3 "Обиходного
  напева", tone 2 "Сокращённого киевского роспева, «Обиход»", each tone 2
  in two layouts, "для большого смешанного – в широком расположении или
  мужского хора" and "для малого смешанного хора – в тесном расположении".

So Smirnov calls his source simply «Обиход»: the common Russian parish
obikhod, with the Court chant for tones 1 and 3–8 and the shortened
Kievan chant for tone 2. Compared with the Dutch (`cmp_smirnov.py TONE
PDF PAGE`; the 2007 files have their music from page 3, tones 2–3 page 1):

- **Tone 2, «Обиход», small mixed choir in close harmony**
  (`01c_na_gv_glas_2_tesn.pdf`): soprano, alto and tenor are the Dutch
  note for note (covers 1.00), bass 0.78, **in the same key (F)**. The
  closest witness found for any tone.
- The 2007 mixed settings of tones 1, 3, 5–8 are the same harmonization
  as the men's set, voiced for mixed choir (in tones 1, 6 and 8 the
  soprano takes the melody line, the alto the Dutch soprano line a sixth
  lower): tone 1 1.00/1.00/1.00/0.82, tone 5 0.80/0.76/0.77/1.00, tone 6
  0.76, tone 8 0.90 (the OCA 0.98). Tone 8 is in F, as the Dutch.

The 2013 tone 4 file can't be read by `ocaread.py` (its fonts have no
Unicode map: every character comes out as U+FFFD); by eye it is the same
arrangement as the 2021 file.

**Which «Обиход»?** Not the Court editions: Bakhmetev (1869), the 1914
Court Obikhod and the azbyka.ru «Обиход» set (a retypesetting of the 1914
edition, see "Other editions") have the same melodies, but not the
cadence turns that Smirnov shares with the Dutch. In tone 1, for example,
the melody at "услыши мя" ends 2…2 7 1 in all three (azbyka.ru in G: A…A
F♯ G G G), where Smirnov and the Dutch go on falling, 2…2 7 1 7 6. So
Smirnov's «Обиход» is a later practice version of the Court chant: the
contemporary Moscow obikhod (see "The Moscow obikhod"), which has these
turns. Kastalsky's Synodal Obikhod, first taken as a candidate, is ruled
out by its texture (open harmony, melody in sixths, per Kustovsky and
Potemkina): *Воскресная служба Октоиха. Гласы 1–8. По
обиходу Синодального хора*, гарм. А. Кастальского *Воскресная служба Октоиха. Гласы 1–8. По
обиходу Синодального хора*, гарм. А. Кастальского (Живоносный Источник,
2005; [shop listing](https://music-festivals.ru/catalog/noti/voskresnaya-sluzhba-oktoikha-glasy-1-8-po-obikhodu-sinodalnogo-khora-garm-a-kastalskogo)),
and the St Petersburg Theological Academy's *Осмогласие на «Господи
воззвах»* (2014, ISBN 978-5-906627-07-0; [description](https://esxatos.com/osmoglasie-na-gospodi-vozzvah-kievskogo-raspeva)),
which has the Court Obikhod (Bakhmetev) in all eight tones and the
Synodal Obikhod (Kastalsky) in tones 1, 2, 3 and 7, in close and open
harmony. Neither is freely online. Searched without result: Smirnov's
own pages (no source named beyond «Обиход»; the tone 4 contents cite
"Обиход 1892 г." only for the dogmatik), ECMR's scanned chant books (no
Synodal edition), and Archimandrite Matfey's manuscript library at
[lavra-noty.ru](https://lavra-noty.ru/rukopisnaia-biblioteka-arhim-matfeia/stixiry-na-gospodi-vozzvax-100004/)
(22 "Господи воззвах" items, all Znamenny, Kievan or monastery chants).

## The Moscow obikhod (Kustovsky & Potemkina 1999; «Живоносный Источник» 1996)

Found 2026-10-23 on azbyka.ru, from Smirnov's «Обиход» onwards.

- **E. Kustovsky, N. Potemkina, *Пособие по изучению осмогласия
  современной московской традиции***, Moscow: издательство Церкви Трех
  Святителей на Кулишках, 1999. On azbyka.ru as
  [the whole book](https://azbyka.ru/kliros/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/posobie-po-izucheniyu-osmoglasiya-e.-kustovskij-n.-potemkina.pdf)
  (87 pp., with the preface) and as
  [one PDF per tone](https://azbyka.ru/kliros/posobie-po-izucheniyu-osmoglasiya-sovremennoj-moskovskoj-tradiczii/)
  (`kust/pdf/osmoglas/glasN.pdf`, page numbers as in the printed book;
  tone 1 is pp. 12–18). Per tone: A. стихирный (refrain, lines 1–4,
  variants, closing line, "Порядок строк"), Б. тропарный, В.
  ирмологический, Г. прокимен. Vector PDFs (Finale; music font
  "Anastasia", Maestro layout, staves drawn as `=` glyphs), read by
  `ocaread.py`.
- The preface (pp. 1–8): the melodies of today's Moscow obikhod were
  fixed in the Synodal books from 1772 on and, for the stichera of all
  tones, in the «Круг церковных песнопений обычного напева Московской
  епархии» (earliest surviving edition 1882, Общество любителей
  церковного пения, written down from Moscow parish precentors), with
  influence of the St Petersburg Court Chapel (L'vov 1830/1848, Bakhmetev
  1869) and of the Kiev and Trinity-Sergius Lavras. Its harmony is
  practice, not an edition: "в современной певческой практике … верхние
  голоса поют мелодию напева параллельными терциями", unlike Kastalsky
  (open harmony, melody in sixths), Smolensky (men's choir, sixths) and
  V. Martynov's *Осмогласие для смешанного хора* (1991; soprano and
  tenor in sixths). The book sets the tones "в наиболее «обиходном», т. е.
  распространенном виде: в четырехголосном тесном расположении …, для
  смешанного состава хора …, с терцовым соотношением сопрано и альта".
  It names the 1996 «Живоносный Источник» series as giving the obikhod
  tones "в одном варианте в конце сборника".
- ***Песнопения воскресной службы***, глас 1–8, Moscow: Православный
  центр «Живоносный Источник» в Царицыне, 1996 (computer typesetting A.
  Gorlov, M. Potokin, M. Smirnov; ed. S. Potokina): texts of the Sunday
  Octoechos with a music supplement; at the end "Образцы обиходных
  напевов", with "Господи, воззвах" through-composed (tone 1 pp. 35–36,
  close harmony, F major, three upper voices on the treble staff). On
  azbyka.ru as scans, one post per tone
  ([tone 1](https://azbyka.ru/kliros/glas-1-voskresnye-pesnopeniya-oktoiha-mosk-reg-pev-seminarii-tsl-smesh-hor/),
  [tone 2](https://azbyka.ru/kliros/voskresnye-pesnopeniya-oktoiha-glas-2-smesh-hor/)
  … `voskresnye-pesnopeniya-oktoiha-glas-N-smesh-hor/`, tone 6
  `…-gals-6-…`). Tone 1 by eye: soprano, alto and bass are the Dutch,
  with the cadence turns; the tenor (E–F … on the treble staff) differs.
  The tone 1 post also has `g1.pdf`, a four-part vector setting of the
  same tone (its URL names the Moscow Regent-Singing Seminary of the
  Trinity-Sergius Lavra): the Dutch in all four voices but for the
  tenor's C where the Dutch has C♯ at the final cadence. There is no
  `g2.pdf` … `g8.pdf`.

The handbook's transposition: tones 1, 2, 4, 6 none; 3, 5, 7, 8 down a
tone (its G major to the Dutch F). Where the Dutch differs from it:

- Tone 3: one added A in soprano and alto (G A B♭ A G F G for G B♭ A G F
  G, m1, 3, 5, 7, 9) and a slightly different cadence in m6, m11; tenor
  and bass identical.
- Tone 4: two bass passing notes fewer (F A D for F G A D, m6, m11).
- Tone 5: small added turns in the upper voices (E–D, C–D in m3, m9) and
  a longer final cadence (m6, m11); bass identical.
- Tone 7: a passing F before the last G in soprano and alto (A F G for A
  G), E–F neighbour notes in the bass.

These are the kind of variants the authors expect between choirs ("иные
традиционные варианты напевов … не считать их «неправильными»"). So the
Dutch adaptation was made from this Moscow practice, either from a
printed witness like the handbook or the 1996 series, or from singing.
